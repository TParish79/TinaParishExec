How I Operate

In a founder-led company the two titles blur, and I've worked both sides of the line. As Chief of Staff, the job is to make the founder more effective. As COO, I carry the ownership and the execution authority. In practice I bring both — the instinct to read what a founder needs before they ask, and the mandate to go build it.

Diagnose Before I Change Anything

Understand the business and the founder

You can't fix an operation you haven't looked at honestly. Every engagement starts with a full audit, not a reorganization.

  • Complete audit of operations, departments, systems, and processes — what exists, what's documented, and what people actually do instead
  • Deep time with the founder on the vision, how the company got to where it is, and what they're protecting
  • Identify the real constraint blocking the next milestone, which is rarely the one everyone is talking about
  • Map dependencies, bottlenecks, and single points of failure before they turn into a crisis
  • Deliver findings straight, including the parts that are uncomfortable to hear

Build What's Missing, Fix What's Broken

Process, systems, and infrastructure

Whatever process is missing, I create. Whatever process isn't working, I rebuild. Same for the systems underneath them.

  • SOP development from the ground up across operations, client delivery, sales, marketing, HR, and administration
  • Workflow design and process documentation that survives the person who wrote it
  • System selection, implementation, and migration — with adoption owned, not assumed
  • Automation of the manual work that quietly consumes a team's week
  • Vendor and tool consolidation to cut cost, overlap, and complexity
  • Infrastructure built to hold at the next size of the business, not the current one

Own the Outcome

Execution and accountability

A COO doesn't advise on the plan. A COO is accountable for whether it happens.

  • Translate the founder's vision into an operating plan with owners, milestones, and dates
  • Org design and reporting structure matched to the stage the business is in
  • End-to-end hiring for senior roles: role definition, screening, interviews, offer, and onboarding
  • KPI frameworks and executive dashboards that surface problems early instead of confirming them late
  • Board-ready reporting, kept current rather than assembled the week before
  • Crisis management and strategic pivots without losing continuity
  • Hold the line on accountability across teams, including when it's unpopular

Read the Founder

The Chief of Staff instinct

The half of the job that never makes it into a job description — and the part that makes the rest of it work.

  • Protect the founder's time, attention, and priorities; treat the calendar as a strategic instrument
  • Own the cross-functional work that doesn't belong to any one team and would otherwise stall
  • Serve as the liaison between the founder and clients, partners, investors, and staff
  • Write and carry executive communications, internal and external
  • Absorb the detail so decisions get made on what matters, not on everything
  • Anticipate what's needed before it's asked for — and handle it quietly
  • Discretion by default

What to Expect

Two questions every founder asks before bringing on an operator: what happens when she starts, and what has she actually delivered.

The First 90 Days

Diagnose, stabilize, build
  • Days 1–30 — Diagnose: Operations, systems, processes, team, and client delivery mapped end to end. Extended time with the founder on vision, history, and the real constraint. Findings delivered straight.
  • Days 31–60 — Stabilize: Fix the processes actively costing money or credibility. Document what currently only lives in one person's head. Land the early wins that buy room for the structural work.
  • Days 61–90 — Build: Org and reporting structure, KPI framework and dashboards, SOP library underway, and a 6–12 month operating plan with named owners and dates.
  • Throughout: A steady founder rhythm and a standing readout, so the state of the business is never a surprise to either of us.
